Searching For Social Justice:
Four Emergent
Phenomenological Themes

Normalization of Academic Racism

  • White values as professional identity
  • Hero worship of "deified" theorists
  • Perpetuation of white ideas of wellness

Searching For Social Justice

  • Minimal inclusion of social justice in curricula
  • Leads to a need for continuing education outside of Master's/Doc programs

Structural Barriers

  • CACREP as a source of academic racism
  • Not allowing the integration of different ways of knowing in favor of "canon" content

Advocacy & Change

  • Risk-free advocacy: advocacy is often too costly for participants without leadership positioning
  • Making room for social justice: need to adapt CACREP standards to add unwritten social justice standards and include current events

Reference: White, E., Nadrich, T., Walo-Roberts, S., Martinez, T., Crawford, C., & Ferguson, A. (2024). Searching for social justice: How counselor educators incorporate multicultural and social justice praxis across CACREP accredited programs. Journal for Social Action in Counseling & Psychology, 16(1), 43–58. https://doi.org/10.33043/y98zc67324
